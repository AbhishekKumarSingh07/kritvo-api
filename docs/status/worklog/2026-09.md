## 2026-09-23

- **Done:** Walked through ADR-0004 (events for field actions, versions for office edits) in plain language, and worked through what "who made this change" tracking looks like end to end: field-action events already carry an actor (`actor_id`, `on_behalf_of`, `channel`) on every entry; office edits (jobs, invoices, roles, etc.) will get theirs from TASK-005's audit trigger, which isn't built yet. That check surfaced a real sequencing gap: the audit trigger needs an authenticated actor to record, but `core/db.py` only ever stamped `app.tenant_id` and `app.customer_id` on a session — never `app.actor_id` — a piece TASK-003 should have added alongside the principal but missed. Fixed as its own small follow-up rather than folding it into TASK-005, since it's principal plumbing (`kritvo-api` PR #49): `app.actor_id` is now stamped on every tenant session, optional like `app.customer_id` rather than required like `app.tenant_id` (empty string, not an error, since scheduled jobs such as the obligation sweeper run with no human caller), sourced from `principal.user_id`. Two new isolation tests. Also checked TASK-005's actual readiness: confirmed it's a leaf dependency (nothing else in the plan depends on it) and that only 2 of its 6 target tables (`roles`, `memberships`) exist yet — `jobs`, `attendance_sessions`, `stock_movements` and `invoices` arrive with TASK-008/010/011 — so building it as a reusable `enable_audit()` Alembic helper plus a CI coverage check (mirroring TASK-002's RLS pattern) makes more sense than manually wiring six tables, four of which don't exist, today.
- **Decisions:** TASK-005 runs after TASK-004, built as a migration helper plus coverage test, not per-table wiring by hand. Its LLD dependency on TASK-002 will be corrected to also list TASK-003 (for the actor) alongside TASK-005's next LLD edit, not as its own commit. ADR-0004 itself is still under review, not yet accepted.
